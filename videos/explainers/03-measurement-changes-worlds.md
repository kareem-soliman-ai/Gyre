# Explainer 03: Measurement changes worlds

**Target:** 3 minutes. **Outcome:** the viewer recognises observer-effect edges.

Narration:

> Most dashboards assume measurement is a window. Sometimes it is an
> intervention. A scan may disturb the thing being measured, alter the causal
> mechanism, train the subject to respond, or degrade the instrument itself.

Visual:

```text
measurand -> instrument -> reading
    ^            |           |
    +------------+-----------+
       world, mechanism, and instrument interference
```

> Gyre makes those return edges explicit. An active station and a quiet
> reference station help separate ordinary change from change caused by the
> measurement process. Neither station is perfect; the point is to make the
> disturbance model testable.

> Before the terminal outcome, useful signals include prediction calibration,
> evidence novelty, movement toward a falsifier, intervention cost, reversibility,
> and whether the world responds as forecast. These are leading indicators, not
> substitutes for the final criterion.

> A human observation is also a measurement. Expertise, fatigue, incentives,
> and taste affect its error. The remedy is not to remove humans or worship
> automation. It is to calibrate both sensors against outcomes and route
> authority according to stakes, uncertainty, recoverability, and demonstrated
> capability.

Close: show active scan changing the organism, then the quiet station recording
the contrast.
