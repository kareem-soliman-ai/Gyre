# Explainer 05: The human-AI control surface

**Target:** 3 minutes. **Outcome:** the viewer can design capability-sensitive
authority without constant interruption.

Narration:

> Human in the loop is too vague. Which human, at which decision, with what
> evidence, and what action menu?

> A human should normally own a decision when stakes are high and the outcome is
> uncertain, when the system is unsure, or when the human has stronger
> demonstrated capability. A system can act when stakes are lower, the outcome
> is clear, errors are recoverable, and it can verify its own work.

Visual: axes for stakes, uncertainty, reversibility, and relative capability.

> When the system is more capable, it should still expose its recommendation,
> certainty, rationale, and the alternatives it considered. When ambiguity
> remains, the interface should say which capabilities the system contributes
> and which judgement it needs from the human.

> For long tasks, negotiate those escalation rules up front. Repeatedly asking
> for low-value confirmations is not collaboration; it is a badly tuned
> controller.

> Capability evidence can accumulate from calibrated predictions, successful
> self-verification, human revisions, task history, and independent outcomes.
> None is perfect alone. The target is not the ideal human-to-AI ratio. It is the
> joint capability of the dyad.

Close: show a menu with `act`, `show alternatives`, `ask human`, `run test`, and
`rollback`.
